# Experiment: the scorecard without `age` (risk R-11)

Both models fitted on **train** with the same recipe (D-016); compared on **valid** (22,500 rows), each at its own break-even cutoff (D-018). Test is not reopened (D-020). Profit is **Illustrative**. Generated by `python -m scorecard.evaluation.age_ablation`.

**Result.** Without age, the 18-29 approval ratio is **0.834** (95% CI 0.811 to 0.857), which clears the four-fifths screen (0.8, **Benchmark**) on the point estimate. The cost falls on 62+: mean PD / bad rate becomes **1.32** (95% CI 1.16 to 1.53), so their risk is overstated.

## Cost: the whole portfolio

`score_auc_for_18_29` = how well the score alone separates 18-29 applicants from older ones (0.5 = no age information). It stays well above 0.5 without age when the other features act as proxies.

| index | features | cutoff | auc | gini | ks | mean_pd / bad_rate | approval_rate | bad_rate_approved | profit_per_10k | score_auc_for_18_29 | profit_per_10k_change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| deployed (with age) | 7 | 549 | 0.8592 | 0.7184 | 0.5616 | 0.9896 | 86.85% | 3.04% | 3,220,667 | 0.7264 | 0 |
| without age | 6 | 549 | 0.8557 | 0.7115 | 0.5574 | 1.01 | 86.16% | 2.98% | 3,217,889 | 0.6598 | -2,778 |

AUC change without age: **-0.0035** (paired bootstrap 95% CI -0.0057 to -0.0011).
